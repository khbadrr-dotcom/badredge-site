Sizing contracts against a trailing max loss

Micros vs minis, dollars per point, and a simple buffer ladder that grows size only when the account can afford it.

8 min read

Choosing between a micro and a mini contract isn't a style preference. It's a risk decision. The same 5-point move on gold is $50 on a micro and $500 on a full contract. Under a $2,000 trailing max loss, that difference is the whole game.

Size from the stop, not the account

Work backwards from the dollar loss you can accept on one trade:

Contracts = (risk per trade in $) ÷ (stop in points × $ per point)

Example: you accept $150 of risk on a gold trade with a 6-point stop. On MGC that's 6 × $10 = $60 per contract, so 2 contracts ($120). On GC, one contract would risk $600, four times your limit. On a small account, micros are often the only way to size correctly.

The buffer ladder

Under a trailing max loss, the right size changes as your buffer (balance minus floor) changes. A simple ladder:

  1. Pick a block size, for example $700 of buffer per contract.
  2. Each morning, contracts = buffer ÷ block, rounded down, with a minimum of 1.
  3. When the buffer grows, size grows. After a drawdown, size shrinks automatically.

The ladder does what discipline often doesn't: it cuts size after losses and only adds it when the account has earned the room.

The block size is the key number. Too small and a normal losing streak breaches the floor. Too large and the account grows slowly. The Contract Sizing tool searches for the smallest block that keeps your breach chance under a limit you choose.

Don't forget costs

Commission and slippage are charged per contract, so they scale with size. One tick of slippage on MNQ is $0.50, but over hundreds of trades and several contracts it adds up. Always test your size with costs included.
