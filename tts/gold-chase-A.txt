So why does chasing feel so right?

Because the candle is the most visible thing on the screen. Your eye goes to it, and the fear of missing out does the rest.

But by the time a big candle has closed, a lot of the move is already in it. You are paying full price for what is left.

The Order Blocks video showed something similar from the other side. Blocks behind the strongest moves held less often. Big moves tend to give some of it back.

There is a quieter reason too. Every chase that works is memorable. The ones that came back to the middle and took the stop are easy to forget.

So what do you do with a big candle?

First, notice the cost. If your stop goes at the middle, check what that is in dollars before you click. On gold, it was often more than a thousand a contract.

Second, remember the base rate. Two in three came back to the middle within four hours.

If you have a different idea, like waiting for that return, test it the same way. I did not test that here, so I am not going to tell you it works.

And log your own chases. The size of the candle, the time, whether it came back, and the result after costs. Fifty of them will tell you more than this video can.

And if you do chase, size it down. A stop twice as far away means half the size for the same dollar risk.

A few honest limits.

This is one year of gold, on thirty minute candles, with one fixed definition of a big candle. A different size rule could change the numbers.

Inside a candle, I cannot see whether the high or the low came first, so the stop always counted first.

The news count only uses the half past eight time. It does not know which report came out or whether it surprised anyone.

And every split, size, direction, session and news, was found after looking. None of it is ready to trade until it is tested forward.

I also only looked at thirty minute candles. A five minute chart or a daily chart could tell a different story.
