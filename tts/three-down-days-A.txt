Why would a market bounce more often, and still lose more?

One possible reason is who is trading on day four.

After three red candles, many traders buy early, expecting the bounce, and place their stops just under day three's low.

If price dips to that low, those stops turn into sell orders, and the dip gets deeper.

On most days the selling dries up and price recovers. That gives the many small bounces.

On some days the selling keeps going. That gives the few big drops.

I cannot prove this from candles alone. But it fits both numbers: the shakeout below day three's low, and the lopsided size of the days.

Here is how I would use this.

A three day losing streak, on its own, is not a reason to buy gold. The odds of an up day are a little better, and the money is worse.

If you already like a long trade for other reasons, expect the fourth day to test day three's low. That happened about two times in three.

A stop placed just under that low is likely to be hit, even on days that end up green.

And size your risk for a bigger move. After a streak, the down days were nearly twice the size of the up days.

If you trade gold through a broker's spot price instead of futures, the moves are the same, but your costs are different. Check them before you test anything like this.

The point is not to fear the fourth day. It is to stop treating it as a gift.
