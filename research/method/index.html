Method: two tracks, one gate.

Most backtests are built to look good. This process is built to find out whether an idea survives data it has never seen, after real costs.

Updated 12 November 2026

Track 1: explore

New ideas are explored on the most recent 12 months (22 Oct 2025 to 21 Oct 2026). Grid searches are allowed here, and anything found here is labelled in-sample. An in-sample result is a hypothesis, never evidence, because the best of thousands of settings will always look good on the data it was picked from.

Before an idea moves on, it has to pass three cheap checks:

  • Gross check, both directions. The raw signal is measured before costs, on the real side and on the flipped side. If both lose before costs, the idea stops.
  • Random-direction null. The whole search is rerun many times with random trade directions, using the same times, stops and costs. The real pick has to beat at least 95% of these random picks. This one check caught every over-fitted strategy on this desk.
  • Walk-forward. Pick the best setting on the first half of the window, then trade it unchanged on the second half.

Track 2: confirm on frozen data

The rules are written down, hashed and committed before the test (pre-registration). They are then run once on four earlier years, 22 Oct 2021 to 21 Oct 2025, that the idea was never tuned on. Nothing is changed after the result. If it fails, that window is spent for that idea.

The Validator gate

A separate reviewer role decides the verdict. The researcher who built the idea does not. The gate checks:

  • Independent rebuild. Trades are rebuilt from raw bars with separate code and must match.
  • Holm correction. When several versions are tested together, the significance bar is raised so one lucky version cannot pass.
  • Minimum meaningful effect and power, set before the run. An edge too small to cover costs and the risk budget does not count, even if it is statistically real.
  • Cost stress. 2x and 3x slippage, and fees +50%.
  • Year-by-year consistency and both unseen sub-periods positive.
  • Concentration. The best 10 days may not carry more than half the profit.
  • Placebo controls. Level-based ideas are rerun on fake levels; confluence ideas on fake zones.

Verdicts are PASS, NO EDGE or INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE (when the test did not have enough trades to rule an edge out). No idea has received a PASS.

Acceptance: a separate, later filter

Even a PASS would still have to meet the desk's practical limits before it could be considered. These limits are a filter at the end, never a research target:

RuleLimit
Win rateat least 75%
Max drawdown, 1 contract$2,000
Profit target$3,000
Trading hours01:00 to 22:00 GMT+3, flat by the end, no overnight holds
Size in tests1 micro (1 contract for platinum), no compounding

Why the 75% win-rate bar is never optimised for. When the random-direction pipeline was run, its own best picks showed 77 to 92% win rates on 12 months. A high win rate with a small target is what a search produces by default. Ranking on win rate selects luck.

Data

1-minute bars for ten CME Globex futures markets, from a licensed data vendor, checked against TradingView. Contracts roll to the daily volume leader. The licence does not allow redistribution, so no raw data is published here, only rules and summary results.
